# Experimental SVGD + conformal GATv2 planner

Normal planning and visualization runs default to **`gnn_cp_autodiff`**.
Goal arrival is a **soft cost**, with `fixed_goal=false` explicitly recorded
in the double-integrator scenarios. Its default cost includes goal, motion,
collision, speed, smooth degree, terminal velocity, control effort, and the
continuous GNN robustness surrogate. Final acceptance requires both hard-graph CP bounds and hard separation
checks throughout the planned motion. Alternate methods require an explicit `--methods` selection.

The default demonstration now uses a **planar double integrator in SI units**.
Historical waypoint-only results are reproducible using
`planning/examples/legacy_waypoint_scenario.json`.

## Double-integrator dynamics and larger scenarios

The optimization variables are acceleration sequences, not independently
movable positions. Each robot starts at its configured position and velocity:

```text
p[k+1] = p[k] + dt*v[k] + 0.5*dt^2*u[k]
v[k+1] = v[k] + dt*u[k]
```

Acceleration is held constant over each interval. All candidate acceleration
vectors are projected onto `||u||_2 <= maximum_acceleration` at initialization
and after every update. Positions and velocities are generated by the dynamics
and cannot be overwritten to force an endpoint. The default input bound is
**1 m/s²**, sampling time **0.05 s**, and communication radius **3 m**.
This is a planar translational drone approximation, not attitude, motor, or
full quadrotor dynamics.

| Scenario | Start-to-goal translation | Steps | Horizon | Plotting workspace |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| `demo_scenario.json` / `double_integrator_large.json` | 10 m | 200 | 10 s | 20 × 10 m |
| `double_integrator_larger.json` | 25 m | 400 | 20 s | 35 × 10 m |
| `double_integrator_largest.json` | 50 m | 640 | 32 s | 60 × 10 m |

Starts are sampled within `[1,9] × [1,9]` m, subject to separation and degree
conditions, rather than the former 0.75-unit box. Prefixes of one reproducible
50-robot pool support different team sizes. The first ten robots form a compact
seed; the sampling constraints can concentrate robots, so the start box is a
sampling region, not a claim that every team fills the entire box. Goals move
the formation 10/25/50 m along x. Workspace bounds are reference extents and
are not hard walls or obstacle constraints. These are unobstructed formation
transfer demonstrations, not difficult obstacle-avoidance benchmarks.

Seeds use minimum-energy acceleration profiles with target terminal position
and zero terminal velocity; noise is added in control space. After projection,
arrival is encouraged by terminal costs, not imposed. Initial and goal
velocities default to `[0,0]` m/s. `maximum_speed = 3 m/s` remains a **soft**
cost separate from the **hard** acceleration-input bound of 1 m/s². Collision separation has a soft optimization penalty plus hard final
acceptance at 0.45 m. Goal completion and stopping at the goal remain soft.
`fixed_goal=True` is rejected for this model because clamping positions would
break the equations. All four planners retain the hard-graph CP filter; CP
acceptance alone is not full task success.

The autodiff planner backpropagates its GNN position gradient through the
rollout into acceleration controls. SPSA probes bounded acceleration sequences
and rolls them out before GNN scoring. There is no optimization loop that
changes positions independently of velocity/control states.

## Legacy waypoint demonstrations

The following examples describe the older waypoint-only experiments.

![SVGD, GATv2, and conformal prediction pipeline](pipeline.svg)

`planning.svgd_cp_planner` is an isolated prototype. A particle contains a short
sequence of 2-D waypoints for every robot. Each future waypoint is converted
to a directed communication graph. The optional `hard` mode preserves the
trained binary radius graph. The default `smooth` mode uses equation (7) of
Capelli and Sabattini, with `sigma = R^4 / ln(2)` by default, weighted degrees,
and a fixed dense off-diagonal edge index.

The experiment compares four SVGD objectives using identical initial particles:

| Run | Cost during SVGD | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `simple` | Goal, motion, collision, and speed | No robustness objective |
| `soft_degree` | Simple costs plus a smooth degree-shortfall penalty | Differentiable connectivity guidance followed by the CP filter |
| `gnn_cp_spsa` | Soft-degree costs plus a GATv2/CP robustness-shortfall penalty | Learned robustness guidance using SPSA, followed by the CP filter |
| `gnn_cp_autodiff` | Soft-degree costs plus a continuous expected-class robustness penalty | Automatic position gradients through equation (7) and the frozen GNN, followed by the hard CP filter |

`soft_degree` is the name used in new terminal output, `--methods`, and JSON
result keys. Some earlier results used the temporary name `cp_penalty` for
this same algorithm. Existing saved files retain their original keys; the
comparison visualizer accepts either name. Earlier `gnn_cp` results correspond
to the current `gnn_cp_spsa` method.

For `soft_degree`, the optimization penalty uses equation-(7) connection weights
and weighted-degree shortfall in the default smooth mode. `degree_weight`
controls this penalty and `cp_weight` is zero. CP predictions are used for
acceptance, not to guide its updates. `gnn_cp_spsa` adds a discrete CP-bound
shortfall penalty controlled by `cp_weight`, with an SPSA position gradient.
`gnn_cp_autodiff` instead adds a continuous GNN surrogate controlled by
`cp_weight`, with automatic position gradients. All methods use hard graphs
for final CP acceptance.

All runs finish with joint CP and separation acceptance, independent of
`cp_weight` and `collision_weight`. With `collision_filter=true` (default),
CP-passing candidates must also maintain `minimum_separation` throughout
every motion interval, including the initial state.
Every candidate seen during optimization must have a conformal lower bound
at least `required_robustness` at every future waypoint. The planner returns
the lowest-cost candidate passing both checks, or `(None, report)` with
`status="no_feasible_trajectory"` if none pass. A scorer is required for every
run. This enforces the predicted-bound condition; the calibration limitations
below still apply to claims about actual robustness.

The `gnn_cp_spsa` run penalizes a trajectory if the **minimum conformal lower bound over its future
waypoints** is below `required_robustness`. For scenario files, this target is
derived from the user-supplied adversary bound as
`required_robustness = 2 * adversary_bound + 1`. `cp_kind="one_sided"` uses the
integer residual score; `cp_kind="lac"` uses the lower endpoint of the LAC set.
The discrete CP cost has no ordinary path derivative. The prototype estimates
the gradient of a Gaussian-smoothed CP cost with two-sided random probes and
applies an RBF SVGD update to the particles. All positive and negative SPSA
probes are submitted together for graph scoring, using bounded GNN minibatches.
This reduces repeated setup without reducing the number of probe graphs. The soft degree term provides a
differentiable connectivity signal. In smooth mode it uses the equation-(7)
adjacency; hard mode retains the original sigmoid degree heuristic.

In both planning modes, GNN/CP certification uses the binary hard-radius
support graph. The checkpoint and calibration records are binary graphs, and
exact r-robustness is defined on that same support. Smooth weighted adjacency
is used only as a differentiable planning surrogate; feeding it directly to
the binary-trained CP scorer would violate calibration exchangeability.

The GATv2 layer uses a scalar edge value both in its learned attention logit
and as a multiplicative attention prior. Thus zero-weight dense edges cannot
send messages. For binary compact graphs this layer is algebraically identical
to the original `jraphx` layer, so existing checkpoints load unchanged.

## Differentiable GNN planner

`gnn_cp_autodiff` uses equation (7) edge weights and weighted node degrees
throughout its optimization graph. It uses a fixed dense off-diagonal edge
index, frozen GNN parameters, and JAX arrays so gradients reach positions.
For each waypoint, its continuous guidance value is:

```text
continuous_lower = sum(class_index * GNN_probability[class_index]) - quantile
penalty = cp_weight * max(required_robustness - min_waypoint(continuous_lower), 0)^2
```

This is a surrogate, not a calibrated CP lower bound. It omits integer argmax,
ceil, and clipping from the optimization path. The waypoint minimum and squared
hinge are continuous and piecewise differentiable; JAX differentiates them.
The one-sided correction comes from the existing hard-graph calibration, and
is only an offset in this surrogate. LAC and infinite calibration quantiles
are unsupported by this new objective and produce an explicit error.

Every population still receives a separate hard-graph CP evaluation. Final
acceptance uses those hard bounds, while cost ranking uses the planner's own
optimization objective. The JSON `planner_report.cost` is that optimization
cost; `total_shared_objective` remains the existing common evaluation cost.
`planner_config` records settings separately for each method.

The binary-trained checkpoint is reused experimentally on fractional edge
weights for guidance; validate or retrain it for weighted inputs before
relying on the learned guidance. The final CP filter remains on binary graphs.
This change does not establish coverage for adaptively selected trajectories.

The implementation maps graphs/trajectories with activation recomputation to
bound reverse-mode memory, rather than keeping all dense graph activations on
the GPU together. Automatic differentiation eliminates SPSA probes but adds
GNN backpropagation; a speed improvement is not assumed.

Run only the new planner from `jax_GNN/`:

```bash
JAX_PLATFORMS=cuda .venv/bin/python -m planning \
  --scenario planning/examples/legacy_waypoint_scenario.json \
  --robots 50 --particles 16 --steps 50 --iterations 5 \
  --methods gnn_cp_autodiff \
  --output reports/filtered_results/11-01/cuda_autodiff_50robots_50steps.json
```

Omit `--methods` to run the default `gnn_cp_autodiff` planner. Pass multiple
method names to compare a subset, or
`--methods simple soft_degree gnn_cp_spsa gnn_cp_autodiff` for all four. The new planner always uses smooth connectivity,
even if `--connectivity-mode hard` selects hard-mode surrogates for other runs.

This launches 75 experiments, each containing the three legacy comparison methods, so
it can take a long time. Edit the three arrays at the top of `run_sweep.sh` to
reduce or densify the grid. With `F=2`, `r_required=5`; runs with 5–8 robots
are retained as infeasible controls and always report `Success=NO`.

The runner creates 16 initial trajectories around a straight-line route using
one fixed random seed. It gives the **same particles** to all four runs and
uses the same SVGD iteration count. The `simple` and `soft_degree` objectives use the GNN only to score candidates for the final filter, without a learned
gradient penalty. After filtering, every selected
trajectory is evaluated with the same graph scorer and, for at most 10 robots,
an exhaustive robustness checker. The table reports CP lower bound, exact
robustness, goal error, maximum waypoint step, minimum robot separation, and
cost under the same full objective. Runtime includes SVGD optimization and final filtering;
loading/calibrating the scorer and post-run evaluation are excluded. The JSON
output records all four selected trajectories and metrics. Use `--help` to
change particles, horizon, iterations, noise, seed, checkpoint, or calibration
file. Use `JAX_PLATFORMS=cpu` if CUDA is unavailable.

Unless `--noise` is supplied explicitly, initial waypoint noise is set to 10%
of `maximum_speed * sampling_time`. Use `--noise-fraction` to change that
fraction. This keeps random perturbations proportional to how far a robot is
allowed to move in one sampling interval.

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    A[Same starts, goals, seed and initial particles] --> B[Simple SVGD]
    A --> C[Soft degree: smooth connectivity guidance]
    A --> D[GNN + CP with SPSA]
    A --> I[GNN + CP with autodiff]
    B --> G[Final filter: CP target and continuous separation]
    C --> G
    D --> G
    I --> G
    G --> H[Lowest-cost passing candidate or no feasible trajectory]
    H --> E[Evaluate selected trajectory]
    E --> F[Compare exact robustness, constraints, goal error and runtime]
```

This is an ablation of the added objectives, not a claim that lower training
cost alone produces a better plan. For a useful result, repeat the comparison
across realistic scenarios and seeds. The current one-sided CP quantile is
zero for the saved checkpoint, and the calibration data omit robustness-zero
graphs, so the reported lower bound is not a safety certificate.

To try your own setting, copy `planning/examples/legacy_waypoint_scenario.json`, replace `start`
and `goal` with your robots' 2-D positions (same number and order of robots),
and replace `config.communication_radius`, `minimum_separation`,
`maximum_speed`, `sampling_time`, and `adversary_bound` with your
measured/planned values. For
example, `adversary_bound: 2` produces `required_robustness: 5`.
The radius, separation, maximum step, start, and goal must use the same length

## Use the planner as a Python module

Example (from the repository root, with the project virtual environment):

```python
from pathlib import Path
import numpy as np
from planning.svgd_cp_planner import ConformalGraphScorer, PlannerConfig, plan

start = np.asarray(...)  # (robots, 2), in meters
goal = np.asarray(...)   # (robots, 2), in meters
particles = np.asarray(...)  # (particles, future_steps, robots, 2)

config = PlannerConfig(
    communication_radius=2.0,   # replace with measured radio/connectivity rule
    minimum_separation=0.5,
    maximum_speed=1.0,
    sampling_time=0.1,          # dt; maximum_step is 1.0 * 0.1 = 0.1
    required_robustness=5,      # 2F+1 for F=2
)
scorer = ConformalGraphScorer(
    Path("checkpoints/full_run_xK0R3I/new_gatv2.msgpack"),
    Path("calib_data.json"),
    config.cp_coverage,
    config.cp_kind,
)
waypoints, report = plan(start, goal, particles, scorer, config, iterations=20)
print(report)
```

This is a planning experiment, not a flight controller. The example distances
are placeholders. The repository has no authoritative dynamics, communication
radius, obstacle map, or planning scenario. The current calibration data have
no robustness-0 graphs, and the selected plan is chosen adaptively from many
GNN queries. Consequently the nominal CP coverage cannot be interpreted as a
guarantee for the selected trajectory. Validate the communication graph rule,
calibration distribution, collision/dynamics constraints, and selected plans
before integrating with a robot system.

## Differentiability and checkpoint limits

The differentiable path in smooth mode is `positions -> equation-(7)
adjacency -> weighted degree -> degree shortfall -> SVGD gradient`.

The hard CP path used by `gnn_cp_spsa` is not end-to-end differentiable. It contains
the hard support condition at `d = R`, class `argmax`, integer conformal
quantile/lower-bound lookup, a minimum over future steps, and particle `argmin`.
It also crosses host NumPy batching. `gnn_cp_spsa` therefore uses
two-sided SPSA probes for its hard-bound penalty. `gnn_cp_autodiff` bypasses
these discrete operations in its continuous optimization surrogate, while
retaining hard CP acceptance. Exact r-robustness evaluation is
combinatorial and is never differentiated.

The gradient audit found these concrete barriers:

| Pipeline operation | Gradient consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Hard `distance_squared <= R^2` and `np.nonzero` | Binary values and a dynamic sparse edge list; no position derivative |
| Host `np.asarray` plus Python graph-record/batch construction | Breaks JAX tracing before GNN inference |
| GNN class `argmax` | Converts continuous probabilities to an integer class |
| LAC comparison and first-true `argmax` | Boolean threshold and discrete set endpoint |
| One-sided CP `ceil`, `clip`, and integer cast | Discrete calibrated lookup/output |
| Minimum lower bound across waypoints | Selects a worst step after the already-discrete lookup |
| Exhaustive r-robustness subset/bit-count logic | Purely combinatorial evaluation |
| Final particle `argmin` | Discrete plan selection, not an optimization gradient |

The hard support test remains in equation (7), but clipping makes the two
pieces meet with zero first derivative at the boundary. It is therefore finite
and differentiable there in JAX while still providing no recovery gradient
from outside the radius.

Equation (7) has zero position gradient at `d = 0`, at `d = R`, and throughout
`d > R`; it cannot attract robots that are already outside communication
range. The branch is evaluated safely by clipping `R^2 - d^2` before `expm1`,
so the inactive exponential cannot overflow under JIT.

The bundled GATv2 checkpoint was trained only on binary edge attributes and
integer degrees. Loading it in smooth mode is structurally compatible, but its
predictions on fractional weights/degrees are out of distribution. Retraining
on weighted graphs, followed by fresh conformal calibration from the same
weighted-graph distribution, is recommended before interpreting those scores.

## Collision-weight trials

Use `--collision-weight VALUE` with `python -m planning` to override the soft
collision cost without changing the scenario file. Goal arrival remains soft.
`python -m planning.tune_collision_weight` reuses the saved 50-robot r=21
comparison's exact geometry, initial particles, seed and optimization budget
while varying only that weight. It saves individual trials and CSV summaries.
The continuous separation checker minimizes pair distance within each
constant-acceleration interval, including interior stationary points.

The [trial report](../reports/trials/11-09/collision_weights_r21/REPORT.md)
records eighteen tested weights. None produced zero violations of the 0.45 m
separation threshold or zero body overlaps with radius 0.2 m. Weight 6,900 is
the lowest tested weight tied for the fewest violating pairs; the trajectories
remain unsafe. The default
collision weight stays at 10.

Use `--particles 50` or `--particles 100` with the tuning script to change
the population while retaining the saved geometry, seed, noise, horizon and
iteration count. Use separate output folders for each population. The default
trial policy includes the hard collision filter; `--legacy-cp-only` explicitly
enables CP-only acceptance for cost experiments.

`--compare-cp-only-output PATH` additionally saves the CP-only selection from
the same optimized populations while running the hard filter. It checks every
CP-passing candidate and records both selections, including null trajectories
when none pass the hard filter. This avoids duplicate GNN optimization:
neither final acceptance rule feeds back into particle updates. Both outputs
share the measured solve time, including collision screening. See the
[large-population comparison](../reports/trials/11-11/large_particle_collision_trials/REPORT.md)
for weights, collision counts, solver times and reproduction commands.

Large double-integrator populations use batches of eight for base-cost
gradients. The CP scorer builds at most about 256 graphs at a time, aligned
to its checkpoint's inference batch size. These bounds limit temporary
memory; every particle and waypoint is still evaluated. Add `--progress`
to the tuning command to print stages and CP/joint acceptance counts for
each population.

## Robot body radius

Double-integrator example scenarios now explicitly set `robot_radius=0.2` m
and a center-distance target of at least 0.45 m. For equal disks, contact
occurs at 0.4 m center distance, and the 0.45 m target leaves 0.05 m between
surfaces. The planner rejects a configured separation target smaller than
twice the body radius. Separation remains a soft cost during optimization. Candidates that violate
the hard final separation check are rejected. No contact-response dynamics
are simulated.

The close-up renderer now uses world-coordinate circles of radius 0.2 m, not
fixed-pixel markers. Historical data can be displayed using an explicit
`--robot-radius 0.2` override. Green goal points and small route-map dots remain
symbolic. The close-up reports negative surface gaps as BODY OVERLAP.

```bash
.venv/bin/python -m planning.visualize_network_closeup \
  --input reports/visualizations/11-09/50robots_r21 --robot-radius 0.2
```

`continuous_separation(..., robot_radius=0.2)` reports physical overlap counts
(<0.4 m) separately from safety-distance violations (<0.45 m). The extended
weight-sweep report includes both, using the user's radius assumption on the
recorded trajectories.

## Joint final CP and collision filter

Final acceptance now requires both the CP target and collision clearance.
`collision_filter=true` is the default in the API and example scenarios.
For each candidate, CP bounds must meet `required_robustness` at every future
waypoint, and all robot centers must stay at least `minimum_separation` apart.
For the current body radius of 0.2 m, the 0.45 m threshold includes a 0.05 m
surface gap. The check includes the initial configuration and the motion
between waypoints, not just the stored positions.

The double-integrator check minimizes pairwise separation over each quadratic
constant-acceleration interval using endpoints and stationary points. Legacy
waypoint plans are checked along straight-line segments. Sampled collisions
are rejected first to avoid unnecessary root calculations. Checks use a
1e-9 m comparison tolerance; this is numerical acceptance under the model,
not an execution/estimation safety certificate.

The collision penalty still guides optimization. Goal arrival stays soft
(`fixed_goal=false`), with no goal-distance acceptance threshold added. Only
candidates passing both final checks enter cost ranking or become the retained
incumbent. If none pass, the planner returns `None` with
`status="no_feasible_trajectory"`. No rejected fallback is returned as a plan.

Reports include `cp_passing_candidates`, `collision_rejected_candidates`,
`passing_candidates` (joint passes), and the accepted plan's `separation_check`.
Double-integrator reports also count rejection reasons. The terminal prints
the rejection counts when no candidate passes. This policy applies to all
four planners, regardless of penalty weights.

Old videos and the 18-weight report used CP-only acceptance and include
colliding trajectories. They are historical outputs, not accepted plans under
the new policy. The tuning helper now defaults to the joint policy and a new
output folder. Reproducing those historical CP-only trials requires the
explicit `--legacy-cp-only` option. Do not mix the two policies in one trial
folder. Accepted-motion visualizers stop with a clear message when no plan
passed; they do not render a rejected candidate as a successful trajectory.
